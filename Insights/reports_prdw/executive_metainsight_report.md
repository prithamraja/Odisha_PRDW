## Top Findings Across the Programme

Across costed activities, overspending against plan is not concentrated in a single block. It is spread across blocks in 27/28 asset categories, with Banking Facilities the only category where the distribution is uneven. The 5,630 activities forming this volume base are distributed as follows: Bhubaneswar 13.7%, Kalimela 8.2%, Rangeilunda 7.9%, Boipariguda 7.1%, Barpali 6.7%, Bheden 6.6%, Laxmipur 6.4%, Baranga 5.9%, Khajuripada 5.9%, Khallikote 5.5%, Tangi Choudwar 5.5%, Balisankara 5.0%, Kalyansingpur 4.3%, Lahunipara 4.0%, Attabira 3.8% and Sheragada 3.5%. These shares describe activity volume, not performance. The central review issue is therefore programme-wide control of planning and expenditure, alongside closer examination of the identified exceptions. No area was given more weight than another.

### Overspending is broadly distributed

The same broad pattern appears in the Gram Panchayat profile. Mean overspending against plan is evenly distributed across blocks in 2/3 social-composition groups; ST-majority Gram Panchayats are the exception. Mean overspending against sanction is also evenly distributed across Gram Panchayats in 2/3 groups, again with ST-majority Gram Panchayats outside the general pattern. These findings call for two lines of review: common checks that apply across the programme, and a more focused examination of sanctioning and spending records for ST-majority Gram Panchayats.

### Cash movement and annual expenditure point in different directions

Activity-linked expenditure increases across every time view examined and also increases over the months in every Gram Panchayat size group. Receipt counts, meanwhile, show a recurring seasonal pattern over quarters across all social-composition groups. These patterns should be read together in reviewing whether the timing of receipts, activity expenditure and cash management is being anticipated through the year.

At the annual Gram Panchayat level, average total expenditure decreases over fiscal years in 3/4 population-size groups. Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above show no clear pattern. Administrative approvals form a peak at 2024-2025 in 2/3 social-composition groups, while Mixed Gram Panchayats show no clear pattern. The departmental review should test whether these movements are consistent across districts and whether approval timing and expenditure timing are aligned.

### Classification remains a material planning issue

For Public Works, Uncategorised records the highest number of activities in 19/20 Gram Panchayats; **Govindapur** follows a different pattern. A dominant Uncategorised portfolio limits the usefulness of category-level scrutiny and makes it harder to compare planning choices with sanctions and expenditure. The review should establish whether these activities genuinely fall outside the available categories or require correction in the records.

### Questions for the next departmental review

1. What explains the broad spread of overspending, and what is different in Banking Facilities and ST-majority Gram Panchayats?
2. Are seasonal receipts and rising monthly activity-linked expenditure being anticipated and reconciled through the GPDP, sanction and payment cycle?
3. Why do Public Works remain predominantly Uncategorised, and which districts and blocks will correct or validate those records?

## Activity Lifecycle - Every Planned Work and Its Money

### Tied Grant Requires Sanction-Level Review

For costed activities, 97.6% of the XV Finance Commission tied grant is recorded against sanitation and drinking water, while Rs 42.61 lakh sits outside those two earmarked purposes. The tied part is earmarked for sanitation and maintenance of open-defecation-free status, and for drinking water supply, rainwater harvesting and water recycling. The residue is recorded against Roads (Rs 31.01 lakh), Education (Rs 8.05 lakh) and Water Conservation (Rs 3.55 lakh). The Department should identify which sanction records carry these amounts and under which head they were approved before deciding how each focus area should be treated.

Within the Rs 17.66 crore of planned tied funding, Sanitation carries Rs 8.82 crore, or 49.9%, against 32.7% of costed activities; Drinking water carries Rs 8.42 crore, or 47.7%, against 18.1% of costed activities. These are the purposes the tied grant is expected to fund; the review issue is the recorded residue outside them, not their leading position.

### Planned Money Is Concentrated on Infrastructure, with Two Exceptions

Theme 6 – Self-sufficient Infrastructure in Village carries Rs 30.07 crore of planned untied funding, or 50.4%, against 32.6% of costed activities. It is the leading theme in 18 of the 20 Gram Panchayats. The exceptions should be taken up individually: **Boipariguda** records Unmapped theme as its leading theme for planned untied funding, while **Kalimela** records Theme 4 – Water Sufficient Village.

The asset record is much less usable. Among Public Works, 5,181 activities, or 55.0%, are Uncategorised, and this is the dominant category in 19 Gram Panchayats. **Govindapur** is the exception, where the distribution follows a different pattern. This does not identify what assets were created; blocks should be asked to complete or correct the asset classification before these works are reviewed by sector.

### Sanctioned and Planned Money Remains Unspent Across Places

Across the activities with sanction records, voucher-linked spending is Rs 5.02 crore below the sanctioned amount. This is spread across districts rather than confined to one or two. Spending against sanctioned amounts ranges from 73.3% in **Ganjam** (Rs 4.61 crore spent of Rs 6.30 crore sanctioned amount) to 92.0% in **Cuttack** (Rs 2.14 crore spent of Rs 2.33 crore sanctioned amount). These figures describe only activities with a sanction record on file, not all planned activities.

The same broad pattern appears against planned cost: spending is Rs 51.96 crore below planned cost and is not concentrated in a small set of blocks across nearly every asset category. Among the clearest cases, **Rangeilunda** has spent 23.1% of planned cost (Rs 2.73 crore spent of Rs 11.84 crore planned cost) and accounts for 7.9% of costed activities; **Kalimela** has spent 23.0% (Rs 1.76 crore spent of Rs 7.65 crore planned cost) and accounts for 8.2%. Even **Baranga**, at 52.9% (Rs 1.24 crore spent of Rs 2.34 crore planned cost), remains well below its planned total and accounts for 5.9% of costed activities. The spread calls for a block-wide review rather than action against only the largest absolute totals.

Status records sharpen the issue. Activities recorded as “Activity Approved” have spent only 0.3% of planned cost (Rs 14.00 lakh spent of Rs 41.75 crore planned cost); they account for 53.9% of costed activities, and their signed spending-minus-plan difference is Rs -41.61 crore. By contrast, ongoing works have spent 85.4% of planned cost (Rs 24.76 crore spent of Rs 28.99 crore planned cost) and account for 37.5% of costed activities. Officials should establish whether approved activities have not started or whether their status and spending links have not been updated.

A separate sanction-record exception is **Chikilli**: it has Rs 0 of sanctioned funding across every fiscal year covered, despite accounting for 5.0% of all activities. The data cannot show whether no sanctions were issued or none were entered.

### Evidence Cannot Be Interpreted Without Missing Decodes

For New/Fresh works, all 6,893 photo uploads are attached to one output category that has no description on file, although that category contains 32.8% of New/Fresh activities. **Chikilli** is the only Gram Panchayat without this common pattern. The Department must supply the output-category descriptions before the photographs can be interpreted by type of output.

At the next review, the officer should ask: Which sanctions place tied grant against Roads, Education and Water Conservation, and under what approval heads? Why does **Chikilli** show Rs 0 sanctioned funding—were sanctions absent or not entered, and which sanctioning offices are responsible for verification? Which Gram Panchayats repeatedly show positive spending above plan, and do the underlying vouchers, plan revisions and geotagged photographs support those cases?

## Monthly Money Flows by Gram Panchayat

### The Clearest Change Is in Recording, Not Cash Movement

Across months, quarters and fiscal years—and across all four Gram Panchayat size bands—the amount of cashbook expenditure linked to planned activities increased. Linking practice improved steeply while total cashbook payments did not show the same increase. This should therefore be read first as more complete recording of links to planned activities, not as growth in spending or delivery.

Over the full window, Rs 24.00 crore was linked to planned activities. Gram Panchayats with 5,000 to 10,000 people accounted for Rs 9.93 crore, or 41.4%, against 40.3% of payment vouchers. Those with 2,500 to 5,000 people accounted for Rs 8.19 crore, or 34.1%, against 38.4% of vouchers. The broad alignment indicates that much of this distribution follows transaction volume. The review should focus on whether the remaining unlinked cashbook payments can be connected to planned activities and whether linking is being completed consistently each month.

### Payments Remain Concentrated Around March

March remains the dominant payment month. The typical monthly outflow of one Gram Panchayat reached Rs 22.47 lakh in 2022-03, Rs 20.12 lakh in 2026-03, Rs 17.20 lakh in 2021-03, Rs 16.29 lakh in 2025-03 and Rs 14.14 lakh in 2024-03; each average covers all 20 Gram Panchayats. A recurring annual pattern was present in all size bands below 10,000 people. Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above were the exception: their monthly payments did not show the same clear annual cycle.

Receipt vouchers also followed an annual pattern across Mixed, SC-majority and ST-majority Gram Panchayats. Calendar quarter 2024-Q1 recorded 395 vouchers, or 10.1%, while 2023-Q2 recorded 43 vouchers, or 1.1%. This describes transaction timing, not receipt values. It nevertheless gives blocks a clear review point: whether receipts and payments are being processed regularly or allowed to cluster near year-end.

### Gram Panchayat Size Changes the Reading of Totals

Across all cashbook payments, Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above recorded Rs 11.62 crore, or 16.9%, against 13.8% of payment vouchers. Those under 2,500 people recorded Rs 6.62 crore, or 9.7%, against 7.5% of vouchers. These totals should not be treated as performance rankings.

The per-Gram-Panchayat monthly figures provide the more useful comparison. The typical monthly outflow was Rs 8.07 lakh among Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above, Rs 5.51 lakh among those with 5,000 to 10,000 people, Rs 4.60 lakh among those under 2,500 people and Rs 3.48 lakh among those with 2,500 to 5,000 people. The largest and smallest bands each contain only two Gram Panchayats, although each average still covers 144 Gram Panchayat-months.

### Sanction and Voucher Patterns Need Targeted Review

Sanction recording changed around 2020-10 for three size bands, when 19 sanctions, or 0.9%, were recorded; the shift for Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above came later, in 2021-01. Annual sanction counts increased among Mixed and SC-majority Gram Panchayats, reaching 485 sanctions, or 23.8%, in 2025-2026, but no clear trend appeared among the two ST-majority Gram Panchayats.

**Bhubaneswar Block** was the principal voucher-count exception. It recorded 2,135 payment vouchers, or 25.0%, and stood apart in five of the six fiscal years; 2024-2025 was the exception, when no block clearly stood out. This is a transaction-volume finding, not evidence of stronger performance, and requires examination of the constituent Gram Panchayats and voucher types.

For five fiscal years, the three SC-majority Gram Panchayats had the lowest typical monthly outflow; 2025-2026 broke that pattern. Across the full window, their typical monthly outflow was Rs 2.31 lakh, compared with Rs 4.38 lakh for the two ST-majority Gram Panchayats and Rs 5.30 lakh for the 15 Mixed Gram Panchayats. The small number of majority-category Gram Panchayats prevents wider generalisation.

### Questions for the Departmental Review

Which Gram Panchayats still carry substantial cashbook payments without links to planned activities, and by when will blocks complete those links?

For Gram Panchayats below 10,000 people, why do payments continue to cluster in March, and what month-wise payment schedule will each block monitor?

Which Gram Panchayats and voucher categories account for **Bhubaneswar Block’s** 2,135 payment vouchers, and what changed there in 2024-2025?

> **Reading note:** this view's time axis is the **cashbook's own 72 months** (April 2020 to March 2026), and a Gram Panchayat month with no transactions is present as a row of zeros rather than absent -- 259 of the 1,440 cells are such months. Two consequences follow. Sanctions are counted by **sanction month**, so the **2,040** sanctions dated inside the window are here and **61** dated outside it are not; the yearly report card counts the full 2,101 by fiscal year, and both totals are correct for their own axis. And **March concentrates payments every year** -- 2,040 of the 8,529 payment vouchers fall in a March -- which is the fiscal-year end, a known property of government cash flow. On activity-linked expenditure specifically: the cashbook's total outflow did **not** grow across these six years (**Rs 15.60 crore** in 2020-21 against **Rs 11.12 crore** in 2025-26), while the share of it carrying a link to a planned activity rose from **2.7%** to **53.2%**, and the number of recorded activity-voucher links rose from **30** to **2,122**. A rise in activity-linked expenditure is therefore a rise in recording completeness unless the cashbook total rises with it.

## Gram Panchayat Report Card by Year

### Cashbook Spending Is on a Downward Path

Average expenditure declined from Rs 26.94 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year in 2020-2021 to Rs 12.58 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year in 2025-2026. The path was not continuous—average expenditure was Rs 20.96 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year in 2023-2024 and Rs 21.29 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year in 2024-2025—but the overall direction was downward.

This pattern appears in three of the four population bands: under 2,500 people, 2,500 to 5,000 people, and 5,000 to 10,000 people. Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above do not show a clear trajectory. Each population band should therefore be reviewed separately rather than treating lower spending as a uniform statewide pattern.

### Planned Money Is Not Converting into Expenditure

The gap between planned cost and expenditure is widespread rather than confined to a few districts or blocks. Across districts, utilization of planned cost ranged from 16.7% in **Kandhamal** (Rs 58.17 lakh spent of Rs 3.48 crore planned cost) to 55.9% in **Khordha** (Rs 3.33 crore spent of Rs 5.95 crore planned cost). **Ganjam** spent 25.2% (Rs 6.00 crore spent of Rs 23.85 crore planned cost), while **Bargarh** spent 35.9% (Rs 5.44 crore spent of Rs 15.16 crore planned cost).

The absolute spent-minus-planned difference was Rs -17.85 crore in Ganjam, which held 21.4% of the 12,704 activities, and Rs -9.72 crore in Bargarh, which held 24.1%. These figures show that the shortfall is not simply located in the district with the largest volume of activities.

Differences are also visible among Gram Panchayats within Bargarh district. **Bheden** spent 22.4% of planned cost (Rs 1.62 crore spent of Rs 7.22 crore planned cost), compared with 57.7% in **Bhatigaon** (Rs 1.24 crore spent of Rs 2.14 crore planned cost). **Bandhpali** recorded 41.9% (Rs 1.40 crore spent of Rs 3.34 crore planned cost), and **Hirlipali** recorded 48.3% (Rs 1.19 crore spent of Rs 2.46 crore planned cost). District review should therefore reach the individual Gram Panchayat-year, not stop at district totals.

### Sanction Records Need Year-by-Year Reconciliation

Administrative sanctions do not follow one common yearly pattern. Across the full sample, 2023-2024 recorded 424 approval records, or 20.2% of the total. In 2024-2025 there were 391 approval records, or 18.6% of the total. The three SC-majority and two ST-majority Gram Panchayats show a 2024-2025 peak, but the 15 Mixed Gram Panchayats show no clear pattern. These small majority groups describe only five Gram Panchayats and should not be generalized beyond the sample.

The recorded difference between spending and sanctioned value also varies materially across Gram Panchayats. Over six fiscal years, **Chikilli** averaged Rs 0 per Gram Panchayat-year, while **Sharagada** averaged Rs -14.30 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year and **Dutimendi** Rs -9.56 lakh per Gram Panchayat-year. Since sanction records cover only about 17% of activities, these comparisons apply only where a sanction is on file. A zero can mean that spending and recorded sanctions match, that both are zero, or that records are missing; the underlying GP-year must be checked before action is taken.

### Evidence Uploads Do Not Move Uniformly with Delivery

Evidence intensity differs across the sample. The two ST-majority Gram Panchayats averaged 81.4 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat-year across 12 GP-years, compared with 73.5 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat-year across 90 GP-years for the 15 Mixed Gram Panchayats and 37.4 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat-year across 18 GP-years for the three SC-majority Gram Panchayats. This describes where uploads are recorded; it does not explain the difference or establish whether evidence is sufficient for the money spent.

Within Bargarh district, average uploads were relatively close—38.8 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat-year in **Bhatigaon**, 34.2 in **Bheden**, 30.7 in **Bandhpali**, and 27.7 in **Hirlipali**—even though planned-cost utilization varied substantially. Blocks should reconcile expenditure, activities evidenced and uploads in the same GP-year rather than treating upload counts alone as proof of execution.

### Questions for the Departmental Review

- Which Gram Panchayat-years account for the decline in expenditure, and why do Gram Panchayats with 10,000 people and above show no consistent trajectory?
- In Kandhamal, Ganjam, Bargarh and the other low-utilization districts, which planned activities remain unspent, and what action is recorded against each?
- For GP-years with activities or payments but zero administrative sanctions or thin photo evidence—including the record with 640 activities and no administrative approvals—did the sanction or evidence not exist, or was it not entered?

## Gram Panchayat Profile - Who Lives There and What They Have

### Spending Below Plan Is Not Confined to a Few Districts

Across the sample, the aggregate spent-minus-planned position was **Rs -51.96 crore**. The gap was spread across all nine districts rather than concentrated in a small set. **Ganjam** recorded Rs -17.85 crore while accounting for 21.4% of the 12,704 planned activities; **Bargarh** recorded Rs -9.72 crore with 24.1% of activities. **Rayagada** recorded Rs -1.24 crore with 3.9% of activities.

The comparison per Gram Panchayat also shows that district totals are not simply delivery rankings. The typical sampled Gram Panchayat in **Khordha** recorded Rs -87.50 lakh per Gram Panchayat, based on three Gram Panchayats, compared with Rs -2.43 crore per Gram Panchayat in **Bargarh**, based on four. **Rayagada’s** figure was Rs -1.24 crore per Gram Panchayat, but it rests on one Gram Panchayat.

This requires district-wide reconciliation of planned and spent amounts, not attention only to the districts with the largest absolute totals. The same even spread was visible among the 15 Mixed and three SC-majority Gram Panchayats; the two ST-majority Gram Panchayats did not show a clear distribution and are too few to support a wider conclusion.

### Sanctioned Amounts and Spending Are Also Out of Step

For activities with sanction records on file, the aggregate spent-minus-sanctioned position was **Rs -5.02 crore**. This amount was again spread across blocks rather than sitting in only one or two. **Sheragada** recorded Rs -85.77 lakh while holding 7.2% of planned activities, and **Rangeilunda** recorded Rs -82.57 lakh with 9.2% of activities.

A zero must not be read as successful matching. **Khallikote** recorded Rs 0 while accounting for 5.0% of activities, and **Chikilli** also recorded Rs 0 per Gram Panchayat. For this measure, zero can mean that no administrative sanction was recorded rather than that spending equalled sanction. The operational question is therefore whether sanctions are absent or whether sanction records have not been entered. The comparison applies only to the sanctioned portion of the activity base.

### Profile Categories Do Not Yet Separate Strong and Weak Delivery

The Mixed category contains 15 of the 20 Gram Panchayats and accounts for 76.0% of the 12,704 activities. Its dominance in totals for population, facilities, plans, sanctions, expenditure and evidence mainly describes the size of that group; it should not be treated as a delivery advantage.

Evidence uploads illustrate why per-Gram-Panchayat comparisons and sample sizes both matter. The average was 488.5 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat for the two ST-majority Gram Panchayats, 441.1 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat for the 15 Mixed Gram Panchayats, and 224.3 photo uploads per Gram Panchayat for the three SC-majority Gram Panchayats. These are descriptions of the sampled places, not evidence of a statewide association with social composition.

Likewise, among Gram Panchayats reporting a panchayat bhawan, the two middle population bands together held most activity volume: 42.9% for 5,000 to 10,000 people and 40.5% for 2,500 to 5,000 people. However, average activity counts, expenditure, cashbook payments and cashbook receipts did not form a clear ordering by population band. The available results therefore do not establish that size, a panchayat bhawan or a reported Common Service Centre goes together with stronger delivery. Nor do they identify a defensible population-adjusted ranking of where water, sanitation, school or health infrastructure is thinnest.

### Questions for the Departmental Review

- What accounts for the spent-minus-planned gaps in **Ganjam**, **Bargarh** and the sampled Gram Panchayats in **Khordha**, and which activities remain financially unresolved?
- For **Khallikote** and **Chikilli**, does Rs 0 against sanction mean that no administrative sanctions were issued, or that sanction records were not entered?
- Can each district present matched Gram Panchayat-level records for planned cost, administrative sanction, expenditure and geotagged evidence, especially where otherwise similar Gram Panchayats show very different evidence volumes?
